# Four-week learning and publishing plan

This is a proposed plan, not a scheduled automation, an event registration or a claim of past activity.

## Week 1 — Reproduce and understand

Read the three English articles, run both examples and identify any explanation you cannot yet give confidently. Keep a small record of the version, actual test results and questions. Revise an explanation only after checking the underlying behavior.

## Week 2 — Rehearse and record

Use the ten slides and presentation script to rehearse the CSV demonstration. Record screen and voice only when you can explain the five diagnostics and the limitations. Review the recording before publishing. Describe a solo recording as a tutorial, not an event talk.

## Week 3 — Teach and learn from questions

Present to an actual study group or community when an opportunity is agreed. Invite questions and record only feedback you really receive. Obtain permission before publishing participant details or recordings. Turn one reproducible question into a documentation improvement or a regression test.

## Week 4 — Maintain something useful

Choose a real issue in the SDK or another project you use. Reproduce the problem, read contribution guidelines, keep the change small and submit evidence with the patch. An improvement to your own repository is not an accepted contribution to an unrelated upstream project.

## Sustainable cadence

Aim for one useful article or maintenance improvement every two weeks, adjusting the cadence to your actual availability. Do not backdate publications or turn several changes on one day into a claim of months of activity. Keep a record of the actual date, resource, change, tests and response.

A long-term publishing or community history is built through repeated real work; this plan and the initial articles are a starting point.
